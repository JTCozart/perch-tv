Install guide

Installing Perch and adding your cameras

Perch is not in the Google Play Store, so it is installed from its APK file. This is called sideloading. It takes a few minutes the first time. Then you give each camera a camera account and add it on the TV.

The download link is always the newest release:

https://github.com/JTCozart/perch-tv/releases/latest/download/perch-tv.apk
Menu names differ a little between TV makers and Android versions. If a setting is not exactly where these steps say, look for the same words under Settings > Apps or Settings > System.

Before you start: allow installing apps

Android only installs apps from outside the Play Store when you give a specific app permission to do it. On newer Google TV devices that option is hidden until developer options are turned on.

  1. Open Settings > System > About.
  2. Scroll to Android TV OS build (or Build) and press Select on it seven times, until a message says you are now a developer.
  3. Go back to Settings > Apps > Security & restrictions > Unknown sources. On some devices this is under Settings > Privacy > Security & restrictions.
  4. Turn it on for the app you will install Perch with, such as Downloader or your file manager. You can turn it off again once Perch is installed.

Method 1: the Downloader app (easiest)

Downloader is a free app built for exactly this. Everything happens on the TV.

  1. On the TV, open the Play Store, search for Downloader by AFTVnews and install it.
  2. Allow Downloader to install unknown apps, as described in Before you start.
  3. Open Downloader. In the address box, type the download link above and choose Go.
  4. When the download finishes, choose Install, then Done.
  5. Downloader offers to delete the APK file. You can say yes; Perch stays installed.

Method 2: send it from your phone

Handy if typing a long address with a remote is a chore.

  1. Install Send files to TV (or a similar file transfer app) on both your phone and the TV.
  2. On your phone, open the download link above to save perch-tv.apk.
  3. Open the transfer app on both devices, choose Receive on the TV and Send on the phone, and pick the APK.
  4. On the TV, open the received file from a file manager that you have allowed to install unknown apps, and choose Install.

Method 3: ADB from a computer

For people comfortable with a command line. ADB is part of Google's free Android SDK Platform Tools.

  1. Turn on developer options on the TV, as in Before you start.
  2. In Settings > System > Developer options, turn on USB debugging or Network debugging. Newer Google TV devices call it Wireless debugging.
  3. Find the TV's IP address under Settings > Network & Internet, on your current network.
  4. On the computer, connect to the TV. With Wireless debugging, first choose Pair device with pairing code on the TV and pair using the address and code it shows, then connect to the address on the Wireless debugging screen (older devices use port 5555): 
    adb pair 192.168.1.50:37425
adb connect 192.168.1.50:5555
  5. Accept the Allow debugging? prompt that appears on the TV.
  6. Download the APK on the computer and install it. The -r flag lets the same command update an existing install. 
    adb install -r perch-tv.apk

Amazon Fire TV

Perch is built for Android TV and Google TV. Fire TV runs a version of Android, so it should work there too, but it has not been tested.

  1. Go to Settings > My Fire TV > About and select your device name seven times to show developer options.
  2. Go to Settings > My Fire TV > Developer options > Install unknown apps and turn it on for Downloader.
  3. Install Downloader from the Amazon Appstore and follow Method 1.

Set up wired Tapo cameras

Tapo cameras only share their video with other apps after you create a camera account on each one. It is a separate user name and password, not your Tapo login, and it only works on your home network.

1. Create the camera account

  1. In the Tapo app on your phone, open the camera and tap the settings (gear) icon.
  2. Open Advanced Settings > Camera Account.
  3. Choose a user name and password, each 6 to 32 characters. Use the same pair on every camera if you like.

2. Find the camera's IP address

  1. In the camera's settings, open Device Info and note the IP address, such as 192.168.1.40.
  2. In your router, reserve that address for the camera (often called a DHCP reservation or static lease). Otherwise the address can change after a power cut and the tile will go offline.

3. Add it in Perch

  1. Open Perch. The first time, choose Add a camera; later, choose Cameras > Add a camera.
  2. Leave Tapo camera (RTSP) selected and enter a name, the IP address, and the camera account. The port is 554 unless you changed it.
  3. For a dual-lens model, choose Telephoto lens to show the second lens instead. Add the camera twice to see both.
  4. Choose Save. Back on the wall, the tile says Live once video arrives.

Wall tiles use the camera's lighter stream so many can play at once. Selecting a tile opens the full-quality stream with sound. On some models the full-quality resolution follows the video quality setting in the Tapo app.

Other RTSP cameras

Choose Stream from a bridge and enter the camera's full RTSP address, including any user name and password your camera needs, for example rtsp://user:password@192.168.1.60:554/live. If the camera has a lower-quality stream, put that in Tile stream address.

Battery and solar cameras

TP-Link's FAQ says the C410, C420, C425 and D230 do not support RTSP. Perch can still reach them the way the Tapo app does on your home network:

  1. Find the camera's IP address in the camera's device information in the Tapo app, or in your router's list of connected devices. Give it a fixed address in your router if you can.
  2. In the Tapo app, turn on Third-Party Compatibility (Me, then Third-Party Services; on older app versions it is under Tapo Lab). It can take a few minutes, or a camera restart, before the camera accepts other apps.
  3. In Perch, open Cameras, then Add a camera, and choose Tapo app password.
  4. Enter a name, the camera's IP address and the password you use to sign in to the Tapo app. Leave Hub device ID empty unless the camera is paired to a Tapo hub.
Experimental. This is TP-Link's own undocumented protocol, so a camera firmware update can stop it working. There is no sound on these cameras yet. A battery camera on a live wall stays awake and drains far faster than usual. The TV must be on the same network as the camera; this does not work over the internet.

Other ways in:

  • Use a bridge. If something on your network can re-publish the camera as an RTSP or HLS stream, add that address in Perch with Stream from a bridge. Tools such as go2rtc and Home Assistant publish streams like rtsp://192.168.1.10:8554/garden. Check that your bridge supports your exact camera model first: support for Tapo battery cameras is limited.
  • Hardwire a doorbell. The Tapo D225, D235 and TD25 offer RTSP when wired with the jumper cable and set to always on. They then work like any wired camera.
  • Choose a wired model for spots you want on the wall. A plug-in outdoor camera on a weatherproof outdoor socket streams all day.

Using the wall

  • Move between tiles with the remote's arrows. Press Select to open a camera full screen with sound.
  • Full screen, left and right step through your cameras and Back returns to the wall.
  • Cameras at the top of the wall lets you add, edit, reorder and remove cameras. The wall shows 16 cameras a page.
  • If a tile says Paused, your TV has run out of video decoders for live tiles. Select it to watch it full screen, or move the cameras you care about most to the start of the list.
  • Perch keeps the screen on while it is open, so the TV's screensaver does not cover the wall.

Updating

Install the newest APK the same way you installed Perch, over the top of the old one. Your cameras are kept. Each release is listed on the releases page with notes on what changed.

Checking the download

Every release includes a SHA256SUMS file. To confirm the APK you downloaded is the one the release published, compare its checksum:

sha256sum perch-tv.apk        # Linux and macOS
Get-FileHash perch-tv.apk     # Windows PowerShell

Only download Perch from this site or from the GitHub releases page. An APK from anywhere else could have been changed.

Troubleshooting

A tile stays on "Connecting" or "Offline, retrying"

  • Check the IP address in the Tapo app still matches, and reserve it in your router.
  • Make sure you entered the camera account, not your Tapo app login.
  • Make sure the TV and the camera are on the same network. Guest Wi-Fi networks usually keep devices apart.
  • A camera that is already streaming to several other apps or an NVR may refuse more connections.

"App not installed" or "There was a problem parsing the package"

The download may be incomplete; delete it and download again. If you previously installed a differently signed copy of Perch, uninstall it first.

The Install button is greyed out or nothing happens

The app you are installing from has not been allowed to install unknown apps. See Before you start.

My cameras are gone after clearing the app's data

Camera details are encrypted with a key that lives only in that TV's secure storage, so clearing data or moving to a new TV means adding the cameras again.
